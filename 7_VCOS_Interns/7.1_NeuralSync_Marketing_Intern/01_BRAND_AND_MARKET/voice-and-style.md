# Voice and style

## Voice

Clear, practical, evidence-led, curious, and operationally specific. Sound like a thoughtful operator diagnosing a workflow, not a hype-driven AI vendor.

## Write this way

- lead with the business symptom in the buyer's language;
- show the workflow and control boundary;
- use short sentences and concrete examples;
- separate fact, inference, and proposal;
- name the next safe step;
- prefer “helps”, “can”, and “designed to” when evidence is limited.

## Avoid

- “revolutionary”, “fully autonomous”, “guaranteed”, or “replace your team”;
- vague “10x” or ROI claims;
- fear-based AI inevitability;
- fabricated urgency or social proof;
- pretending a demo is live production;
- leading with tools, model names, or architecture before the workflow problem.

## Standard CTA

Primary direction: **Request a NeuralSync operating-system demo.**

Do not state a fixed demo duration or booking link until recorded in the decision log. “DM SYNC” is a testable secondary CTA, not a default.

## Claim pattern

`Observed problem → workflow hypothesis → control boundary → evidence state → next step`
